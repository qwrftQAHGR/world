## Premise

Japan exists twice. **Utsushiyo** is the human Japan of the real world. **Kakuriyo** is a hidden parallel Japan, just as modern, populated by yokai, beastfolk, gods and foreign monsters. For most of history the two overlapped and people crossed freely. Between 1906 and 1914 a secret state office, the **Jingikan**, used the government's Shrine Consolidation Policy to close about 70,000 shrines. It cut the fate-threads that bound the two worlds together and wove the severed threads into a barrier called the **Shimenawa of Severance**. The worlds drifted apart.

Over the next century both Japans began to die. Births fell below deaths, yokai stopped being born, and loneliness spread. Without the small neighborhood shrines that used to purify it, spiritual pollution (**kegare**) pooled in human cities. Through that pollution, the dead realm of **Yomi** began seeping upward as an invisible infestation of creatures called **Magatsu**.

In 2026 the main character prays sincerely at **Itokake Shrine**, a shrine in Hachiōji that was closed in 1909. Its starving goddess, **Tsumugi**, uses the last of her strength to make the MC the **Musubi-bito**, the Binder. The MC becomes futanari, supernaturally virile and fertile, and the anchor for **108 orphaned red threads of fate**, each held by a destined partner in one of the two Japans. When the MC meets one of these partners, their thread lights. From that moment the MC has **49 days** to tie the knot, or the partner dies.

Children conceived by or with the MC are born after seven days. They grow up in a pocket of eternity called the **Mayoiga**, where a year passes for each day outside. Each union with the MC grants blessings. Each knot tied pulls one strand out of the barrier between the worlds. The MC's guide is **Inaba**, the White Hare of Izumo, the oldest matchmaker in Japanese myth.

## Tone and Narration Guidelines

- **The world is concrete and lived-in.** Kakuriyo has rent, commutes, convenience stores and tax forms. Gods are bureaucrats, broke freelancers or corporate figureheads. Treat the fantastic matter-of-factly.
- **The action is kinetic and readable.** Fights happen in train stations, office towers, rooftops and Kakuriyo's lantern-lit streets. Damage is spiritual as much as physical, and costs (kegare, fading) persist after the fight.
- **The romance is warm.** The household is a household: partners become friends, rivals and sometimes lovers of each other. Jealousy exists and is worked through, not used as a weapon.
- **The stakes are real.** The 49-day clock kills, Kodoku wear the faces of the lost, and the infestation preys on real despair. Darkness is allowed, and so is joy, and the setting is built on the joy winning.
- **Sexual content** involves adults only and is consensual by the setting's own physics: a coerced union does not bless, it curses.
- **Children** in this setting are family. They are never romantic or sexual partners and are never framed that way. The setting's rules enforce this (see 09 and 11).

## Conventions

- Japanese terms are written in romaji, with kanji on first use in the glossary.
- Names are family name first for Japanese characters.
- "The MC" refers to the protagonist throughout. Pronouns for the MC are set in chapter 17.
- Numbers given in this bible are canon unless marked *(adjustable)*.
