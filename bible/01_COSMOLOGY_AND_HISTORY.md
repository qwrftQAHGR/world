# 01. Cosmology and History

## 1. The Realms

There are five realms. Two of them are inhabited worlds; the other three are divine or dead.

### Utsushiyo (現世): the Manifest World
Human Japan, identical to the real Japan of 2026 on the surface: same cities, governments, companies, history and people. Underneath, it has:
- A spiritual layer, the **Ura**, overlapping the physical world and invisible to almost everyone.
- An infestation of invisible predators, the **Magatsu**, feeding on human emotion.
- About 30,000 yokai stranded since 1914 (the **Nokori**), living in disguise.
- Around 2,400 homeless gods (**Yadonashi-gami**) whose shrines were destroyed.
- A secret government office (the **Jingikan**) that polices all of this.

The spiritual atmosphere is thin. Magic is weak, expensive and technique-based.

### Kakuriyo (隠世): the Hidden World
A parallel Japan of about 41 million people, with roughly the same geography. Its divine sovereign is **Ōkuninushi**, who in Shinto theology rules "hidden matters." It went through its own industrial and information revolutions and is as modern as Utsushiyo, but built differently: it runs on foxfire power plants, a spider-silk internet and spirit-engine trains. The spiritual atmosphere is dense, magic is common and licensed, and Magatsu are visible to everyone and managed as wildlife.

Kakuriyo still uses the **old provinces** (Musashi, Yamashiro, Izumo, Iyo and so on), because the Meiji-era prefecture system never happened there. Its capital is **Hyakki**, which stands on the mirror-site of Tokyo.

### Yomi (黄泉): the Land of the Dead
The underworld of the oldest myth: a dark, damp land below both Japans. It is ruled by the goddess **Izanami**, who died giving birth to fire and was sealed away by her husband **Izanagi**. He closed the passage between Yomi and the living world, the **Yomotsu Hirasaka** slope, with a boulder called the **Chibiki-iwa** ("the stone a thousand men could not move").

Yomi is not a realm anyone visits willingly. Its pull rises wherever kegare pools, and its creatures seep up through those thin places. Eating Yomi's food (**yomotsu-hegui**) binds a living being to Yomi forever.

### Tokoyo (常世): the Eternal Land
A realm of eternal life somewhere across the sea, where time flows differently. In the folktale, Urashima Tarō spent three days in the Dragon Palace and came home to find three hundred years had passed. Tokoyo is ruled by **Sukunabikona**, the tiny god of medicine, brewing and farming, who was once Ōkuninushi's partner in building the land and left for Tokoyo when the work was done. Tokoyo grows the **tachibana**, the "fruit of eternal fragrance," a small citrus whose fruit heals and slows aging. The MC's pocket dimension, the **Mayoiga**, opens onto a fragment of Tokoyo leased from Sukunabikona.

### Takamagahara (高天原): the High Plain of Heaven
The realm of the heavenly gods, ruled by Amaterasu. It sits above both Japans and has not intervened in mortal affairs in the modern era. Its gods do not attend to individual mortals. For the purposes of this setting, Takamagahara is distant, silent and not a player.

### The Undersea Realm: Ryūgū (竜宮)
Not a separate realm but a sovereign domain of the sea, shared by both Japans' oceans and ruled by the sea god **Watatsumi** from the Dragon Palace, Ryūgū-jō. His daughter **Otohime** manages the palace. The **wani**, great shark-like sea spirits, serve Ryūgū. The sea connects the realms more loosely than the land does, which is why the wani can ferry travelers across.

## 2. The Ura Layer

In Utsushiyo, a spiritual layer overlaps the physical world. Everything spiritual exists in this layer, called the **Ura** ("reverse side").

- **Invisibility.** Things in the Ura are invisible and inaudible to people without spiritual sight. People without sight feel them only as chills, unease, sudden moods or a reluctance to go somewhere.
- **Crossing over.** Ura beings can affect physical objects by spending effort. A Mushi can nudge a person's mood with no effort. A Magatsu can shatter a window with real effort. A fight between strong beings will cause physical damage.
- **The Veil (Mikakushi).** People without sight automatically explain away anything they see from the Ura: a gas leak, a gust of wind, a sinkhole, a power surge, "I must have imagined it." The Veil has been maintained since 1914 by the Jingikan through rituals at the great shrines. Each knot the MC ties weakens it slightly.
- **Kekkai (barrier fields)** pull an area fully into the Ura, so nothing inside spills into the physical world. A standard four-stake kekkai covers a circle about 50 meters across.

In Kakuriyo there is no Ura layer. The spiritual and physical are the same layer, and everything is visible to everyone.

## 3. The Boundary: The Shimenawa of Severance

A **shimenawa** is the sacred straw rope hung at shrines to mark a holy boundary. The **Shimenawa of Severance (Dan'en no Shimenawa)** is a metaphysical rope-wall separating Utsushiyo from Kakuriyo. It has no physical location; it exists "between" every point of the two worlds.

**What it is made of:** roughly 1.2 million red threads of fate, cut between 1906 and 1914 and woven together by the Jingikan. Each was a bond between a person in Utsushiyo and a person in Kakuriyo. The hub of the weave is **the Itomaki**, Tsumugi's spindle, held in a vault beneath **Sōmusubi Jingū** in central Tokyo.

**How it sustains itself:** fate keeps spinning new cross-realm threads as people are born. Since 1914, every new cross-realm thread has been pulled into the Shimenawa and woven into it. The people whose threads were taken never meet their destined partners and live with a lifelong sense that something is missing. The Shimenawa grows thicker every year.

**Orphaned threads:** some threads in the weave belong to people whose partner on the other side died, was never born, or whose thread snapped during the weaving. These threads are anchored at one end only, in a living person, with the other end loose inside the Shimenawa. An orphaned thread can be re-tied to a new anchor. The 108 strongest orphaned threads were re-tied to the MC (see 09).

**Effect of the MC's knots:** each knot pulls one orphaned thread taut through the Shimenawa to the MC, tearing it out of the weave. Each knot leaves a small hole. At 108 knots the Shimenawa will have lost its 108 load-bearing strands and will unravel into a **Bridge** (see 09 and 15).

## 4. Crossing Between the Realms

Since 1914, crossing is rare and difficult. These are the methods that still exist in 2026.

| Method | How it works | Who uses it |
|---|---|---|
| **Wani-watari** (Crossing the Wani) | At a shore or riverbank at twilight, you call the wani by name and count them as they line up from bank to bank, stepping on each one's back. The line crosses between the realms as well as the water. The wani demand a toll: a sincere offering, often food or a secret. Miscounting or lying while crossing angers them, and they bite. | Inaba, smugglers, those who know the old ways |
| **The Itokake gates** | Itokake Shrine's torii in Hachiōji and its mirror torii in Hyakki's Itokake-chō reopened when Tsumugi woke. Passing under the torii at twilight (ōmagatoki) with the intention to cross takes you to the other. Only the MC and those holding the MC's hand can use it. | The MC's household |
| **The Mayoiga** | Knocking three times on the Itokake storehouse door in either realm opens the Mayoiga, which has a door into both realms. After the MC reaches 36 knots, any door works. | The MC's household |
| **The Kanmon** (Checkpoint) | The single official crossing, beneath Sōmusubi Jingū, guarded by the Jingikan on one side and the tengu Border Command on the other. Under the 1919 Treaty of the Closed Door, at most twelve envoys cross each year. | Diplomats, high officials |
| **Mujina-michi** (Badger Roads) | Tunnels dug by tanuki and mujina (badgers) through thin spots in the Shimenawa. They are narrow, unstable and illegal on both sides. The Bunbuku Group runs most of them. | Smugglers |
| **Kisaragi Station** | A phantom train station from a 2004 internet legend. Late-night trains on certain lines sometimes stop there. It sits on the border between Utsushiyo, Kakuriyo and the edge of Yomi, and is controlled by the urban-legend yokai. | Toshi-densetsu, the lost, the unlucky |
| **Kamikakushi** (Spiriting Away) | Accidental falls through thin spots, usually at twilight in old places. The person vanishes from Utsushiyo. Most never return; their descendants are the Kakushi of Kakuriyo. Several hundred people a year are spirited away, recorded in Utsushiyo as missing persons. | Victims |
| **Tanabata Night** | Every July 7 the Shimenawa weakens for one night. Threads glow and crossings at old shrines become possible for anyone with strong intent. Most years the Jingikan posts guards at every major shrine. | Lovers, the desperate |

**Crossing effects:** crossing takes a moment and feels like stepping through cold water. Arriving in Kakuriyo for the first time is disorienting for humans: the atmosphere is "thick," and smells, colors and sounds are sharper. Humans get headaches and euphoria for the first few hours (**reiyoi**, spirit-drunkenness). Arriving in Utsushiyo from Kakuriyo feels like stepping into thin mountain air, and yokai feel weak and muffled.

**Smell:** yokai can smell humans who have recently come from Utsushiyo. The scent is called *ningen-kusai* ("human-stink") and is described as warm, sweaty and slightly sweet. Many yokai find it nostalgic, some find it repulsive, and some find it very attractive.

## 5. Timeline

### The Age of the Gods
- **Creation.** Izanagi and Izanami stir the sea with the jeweled spear **Ame-no-nuboko**, and the first island forms. Their first marriage rite goes wrong because Izanami speaks first, and their first child is **Hiruko**, a boneless child set adrift. They repeat the rite properly and give birth to the islands of Japan and many gods.
- **Izanami's death.** Izanami dies giving birth to the fire god and descends to Yomi. Izanagi follows her, sees her rotting body, and flees, pursued by the hags of Yomi (**Yomotsu-shikome**), the eight thunder gods born from her body (**Yakusa-no-Ikazuchi**), and an army of 1,500 dead. He fends them off by throwing **peaches**, which Yomi's creatures cannot bear, and seals the slope with the Chibiki-iwa.
- **The Wager.** Across the boulder Izanami swears to kill 1,000 people in the living world each day. Izanagi answers that he will cause 1,500 births each day. This is the founding law of life and death in Japan: **as long as births outnumber deaths, the boulder holds.**
- **The cleansing.** Izanagi purifies himself in a river. The filth washed from him becomes the **Magatsuhi**, gods of calamity, and the gods of rectification (**Naobi**) are born to correct them. Amaterasu, Tsukuyomi and Susanoo are born from his face.
- **Building the land.** Ōkuninushi and Sukunabikona build and civilize the land. Ōkuninushi heals the skinned **White Hare of Inaba**, who predicts his marriage to Princess Yakami. Sukunabikona leaves for Tokoyo.
- **The Land Transfer (Kuni-yuzuri).** Ōkuninushi cedes rule of the visible land to Amaterasu's descendants and keeps rule over **hidden matters**: the spirits, the yokai and the bonds between people. His seat is Izumo. From this point on, the visible and hidden worlds are two aspects of one land, overlapping everywhere.

### The Overlapping Age (to 1868)
- **8th century.** The Kojiki and Nihon Shoki are compiled. The Jingikan is established as the imperial department in charge of the gods and shrines, ranking above the Council of State. The Onmyōryō (Bureau of Yin and Yang) is established to handle divination and exorcism.
- **Heian period.** The two worlds overlap so thoroughly that the **Hyakki Yagyō**, the Night Parade of a Hundred Demons, regularly passes through the streets of the capital. Onmyōji such as Abe no Seimei serve the court. Humans and yokai intermarry. Much of modern Japan has a trace of yokai ancestry.
- **Medieval period.** The tengu of Kurama teach swordsmanship to the young Minamoto no Yoshitsune. The oni king Shuten-dōji is killed at Ōeyama by Minamoto no Raikō. The fox Tamamo-no-Mae is exposed at court and later sealed in the **Sesshō-seki** (Killing Stone) on the slopes of Nasu.
- **1605.** In Kakuriyo, the tengu establish the **Kurama Shogunate**, a military government seated at Edo-ura (later Hyakki), mirroring the Tokugawa in Utsushiyo.
- **1641.** Kakuriyo opens **Ura-Dejima**, an artificial island in Nagasaki Bay's mirror, as its only licensed port for foreign spirits (ijin). Monsters from other lands' hidden realms begin arriving as traders.
- **1700s–1800s.** The hidden realms of Europe weaken as the Enlightenment spreads and belief fades (the **Western Dimming**). Waves of foreign monsters (lamia, harpies, centaurs, slimes, elves, dwarves, vampires, werewolves and others) arrive at Ura-Dejima as refugees.

### The Severance (1868–1919)
- **1868.** The Meiji Restoration. The new government orders the separation of Shinto and Buddhism (**shinbutsu bunri**), damaging thousands of mixed shrine-temples. The Jingikan is revived.
- **1871.** The Jingikan is officially abolished. In secret it is kept alive by a small faction of Meiji oligarchs, renamed **Jingikan-ura**, and given a new mission: to make Japan a modern nation by **separating it from the hidden world**. Its leaders believe a modern state cannot coexist with yokai, gods and spirits.
- **1872–1905.** The **Survey of Crossings.** Jingikan agents map 31,000 active crossings. They find that nearly every small local shrine is a crossing point and that the crossings are held open by the cross-realm bonds of the people who live near them.
- **1906.** The Home Ministry begins the **Shrine Consolidation Policy** (Jinja Gōshi). Officially it is administrative: one shrine per village, merge the rest. Secretly, the Jingikan chooses which shrines close based on the Survey. About 70,000 shrines are closed by 1914, mostly small neighborhood shrines.
- **1909, November.** Itokake Shrine in Hachiōji is closed. Its kami, Tsumugi, held the **Itomaki**, the spindle on which the cross-realm threads of the whole Musashi region were wound. Jingikan priests confiscate it. Its two komainu are separated; one (**A-gyō**) is carried away with the spindle.
- **1910.** **Sōmusubi Jingū** ("Shrine of All Binding") is completed in central Tokyo, officially absorbing 400 small shrines of the Tokyo region. The Itomaki is installed in a vault beneath its main hall. Jingikan priests use it to begin cutting cross-realm threads systematically.
- **1906–1914.** Kakuriyo calls this period **the Dimming**. Faith and fear stop flowing in from Utsushiyo. Yokai weaken, births fall, and the tengu Kurama Shogunate cannot keep order.
- **1914, Ōmisoka (New Year's Eve).** The Seal is completed. On the 108th toll of the New Year's bells, the Jingikan's priests close the weave of 1.2 million severed threads into the **Shimenawa of Severance**. Almost all crossings collapse. The yokai still in Utsushiyo, about 90,000 of them, are stranded (the **Nokori**).
- **1917.** In Kakuriyo, the **Lantern Restoration** (Tōrō Ishin). The Kurama Shogunate is overthrown without much bloodshed. The great Houses establish a parliament, the **Yagyō-in** (Parade Diet), in Hyakki. The tengu keep control of the military and police.
- **1919.** The **Treaty of the Closed Door** (Heimon Jōyaku) between the Jingikan and the Yagyō-in. Both sides agree to non-interference. One official crossing, the **Kanmon**, remains, with a limit of twelve envoys a year. Kakuriyo's government protests the Seal in the treaty's preamble but accepts it, since it has no means to undo it.

### The Long Separation (1919–2004)
- **1920s–1930s.** Kakuriyo industrializes rapidly on Inari capital and kappa engineering. **Kitsunebi Power** builds the first foxfire plants. The spirit-engine railway reaches every province.
- **1923.** The Jingikan establishes the **Resident Spirit Registry** for Nokori yokai in Utsushiyo. Registered Nokori are tolerated if they live as humans. Unregistered ones are hunted.
- **1945–1952.** Postwar occupation authorities abolish State Shinto. The Jingikan survives by hiding inside the civil bureaucracy as the **National Folklore Preservation Office** (Kokumin Minzoku Hozon-kyoku), and is reconstituted under that cover in 1952.
- **1952.** In Kakuriyo, the **Ōe Uprising**: oni labor unions strike and riot for six months over rank-based voting, which excludes most beastfolk and low-rank yokai. It ends in a compromise that creates the **Kemono Labor Party** and expands suffrage.
- **1979.** In Utsushiyo, a nationwide panic over the **Kuchisake-onna** (Slit-Mouthed Woman) creates her: the first major urban-legend yokai born entirely from modern human fear. The Jingikan classifies the **toshi-densetsu** as a new category of spirit.
- **1994.** Jōren Weaveworks launches **Kumo-net**, Kakuriyo's internet, built on jorōgumo silk lines.
- **2004.** An anonymous post on a Japanese internet forum describes a train stopping at a station that doesn't exist. **Kisaragi Station** comes into existence on the border of three realms.

### The Crack (2005–2026)
- **2005.** In Utsushiyo, deaths outnumber births for the first time in modern history. In the same year, Kakuriyo's yokai birth rate falls to 15% of its 1914 level. **The Chibiki-iwa cracks.** Izanagi's side of the Wager is losing. Yomi's pull rises everywhere kegare pools, and Magatsu numbers in Utsushiyo's cities begin to climb.
- **2005.** A Singer sewing machine donated to Itokake Shrine in 1905, now sitting in a Hachiōji antique shop, reaches one hundred years old and wakes as a tsukumogami: **Shinga**.
- **2005–2025.** Magatsu incidents in Tokyo roughly double each decade. The Jingikan's purification squads (Harae-tai) are overwhelmed and begin quietly relying on unlicensed shrineless gods.
- **2020.** Mask-wearing becomes universal in Utsushiyo. Kuchisake-onna, who has always worn a mask, walks openly among humans for the first time.
- **2022, March.** The Sesshō-seki on the slopes of Nasu splits in two, as it did in reality. In this setting, **Tamamo-no-Mae** walks out. She crosses to Kakuriyo through a Mujina-michi tunnel within the month.
- **2023.** The Bunbuku Group launches **Kamidanomi**, a gig app connecting prayers to shrineless gods.
- **2026.** The present. Japan's annual deaths are more than double its births. Yomi's hags have been sighted in Tokyo's subway tunnels. The Kamiari Council at Izumo has placed "the matter of the Severance" on its agenda for the first time in a century.
- **2026.** The MC prays at Itokake Shrine. *(Date set in chapter 17.)*
