# 04. The Magatsu Bestiary

## 1. Origin and Ecology

**Magatsu (禍つ, "calamity-things")** are creatures that form from pooled kegare, animated by Yomi's pull. They are named after the Magatsuhi, the gods of calamity born from the filth Izanagi washed off after returning from Yomi.

### How They Form
1. Humans constantly give off small amounts of kegare: stress, envy, despair, exhaustion, loneliness.
2. Before 1906, Japan's 200,000 neighborhood shrines purified the kegare of the streets around them, invisibly and continuously.
3. The Shrine Consolidation removed about a third of that network. The remaining shrines cannot keep up. Kegare now **pools** in cities, especially in places of concentrated misery: train stations, overtime offices, empty housing blocks, hospitals, internet cafés.
4. Where kegare pools deeply enough, the ground thins toward Yomi. Since the Chibiki-iwa cracked in 2005, Yomi's pull has been strong enough to **animate** these pools. The kegare clots into living things.
5. Magatsu feed on the emotions that produce kegare, amplify them in their victims, and produce more kegare, which breeds more Magatsu.

### Why People Don't Notice
In Utsushiyo, Magatsu live in the Ura and are invisible to all but seers. Their effects look like ordinary life: bad moods, burnout, depression, rage, accidents, "bad places," lonely deaths. A significant share of modern Japan's quiet misery is Magatsu-amplified. It is not caused by them, but made worse.

### What Starves Them
- **Strong bonds.** Mushi die off in happy households. Tsukimono lose their grip on people who are truly loved. Magatsu avoid places full of laughter and connection.
- **Purification.** Salt, shrine rites, bells, purified water, the Ōharae.
- **Peaches.** All Yomi-born things recoil from peaches, peach wood and peach pits. This comes from the myth of Izanagi's escape.
- **Sunlight** weakens Tiers 2–4 by about a third but does not stop them.

### In Kakuriyo
Kakuriyo also has Magatsu, from its own kegare and from Yomi's seepage, but its dense reiatsu makes them **visible to everyone**, so they are managed as wildlife:
- Tengu **rangers** cull larger Magatsu in the mountains and the outskirts of cities.
- Apartment windows have **Mushi-proof netting**. Convenience stores sell Mushi traps made by kappa.
- **Natural predators** make careers of it: baku (dream-eaters) run clinics, nekomata clans work as exterminators, and some tengu hunt Magatsu for sport.
- Kappa have learned to **refine** dead Magatsu into low-grade fuel (see 13 for where Kitsunebi Power's extra fuel comes from).

Kakuriyo has never had an infestation like Utsushiyo's, because its people can see the problem and deal with it.

## 2. Tier 1: Mushi (蟲)

Spirit-bugs that ride inside or on people and nudge their moods. **Every human carries some.** A typical office worker carries 3–8. Based on the real Japanese folk belief that "bugs" inside the body cause moods, preserved in idioms like *mushi no idokoro ga warui* ("the bug is in a bad place," meaning to be in a foul mood) and *kan no mushi* ("the tantrum bug").

Mushi are killed by salt, bell sounds, a sharp clap at a shrine, a cat's swipe, or a flick from anyone with sight. They die off naturally in happy homes. The MC's children kill Mushi by touch.

| Name | Appearance | Where it rides | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kan-no-mushi** (癇の蟲, tantrum bug) | Red centipede, finger-length | Spine | Irritability, sudden temper, snapping at people |
| **Fusagi-mushi** (塞ぎ蟲, gloom bug) | Grey moth with heavy wings | Sits on the chest | Heaviness, low mood, not wanting to get up |
| **Netami-mushi** (妬み蟲, envy bug) | Green beetle | Behind the ear | Envy, comparison, scrolling other people's lives |
| **Hara-no-mushi** (腹の蟲, belly worm) | Pale grub | Stomach | Resentment that sits in the gut; stress eating |
| **Yoru-mushi** (夜蟲, night gnat) | Swarm of tiny black gnats | Around the head and screens | Insomnia, doomscrolling, 3 a.m. anxiety |
| **Kuchi-mushi** (口蟲, mouth larva) | White larva | Under the tongue | Saying cruel things one doesn't mean |
| **Sabishi-mushi** (寂し蟲, loneliness glowworm) | Pale green glowworm | Over the heart | Loneliness, withdrawing from people. **The larval form of the Kodoku line** (see Apex). |
| **Nemu-mushi** (眠蟲, drowse bug) | Soft grey caterpillar | Eyelids | Exhaustion, nodding off at the wheel |

**Mushi swarms:** where many people carry many Mushi, such as a crowded rush-hour train or an overtime floor, the Mushi jump between hosts and multiply. A packed Chūō Line train at 8 a.m. may carry several thousand.

## 3. Tier 2: Tsukimono (憑き物)

Parasites that latch onto one person and grow on them over weeks or months, feeding on and amplifying a single emotion until it dominates the host's life.

- **Visible to seers** as a creature clinging to the host's back, wrapped around them, or riding their shadow.
- **Removing one:** an exorcist can drive a Tsukimono off with a rite (risky for the host), kill it (often injuring the host), or starve it by fixing the host's life (slow). **The MC can Unravel a Tsukimono** by pulling its anchor-thread, removing it cleanly with no harm to the host (see 12). No exorcist can do this.
- **Once removed,** the host feels as if a fog lifted and often cries for no clear reason.

| Name | Appearance | Effect on host | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shigarami** (柵, entanglement) | Grey vines wrapping the host's limbs and throat | Obsessive overwork, inability to say no, guilt over resting. A leading contributor to overwork deaths (karōshi). | Common in Shinjuku and Marunouchi offices |
| **Gaki-tsuki** (餓鬼憑き, hungry-ghost possession) | Starved, pot-bellied grey imp riding the shoulders | Addiction: gambling, pachinko, drinking, gacha games, compulsive spending | Named for the Buddhist hungry ghosts |
| **Kagebōshi** (影法師, shadow-master) | The host's shadow, slightly wrong: it moves a moment late and smiles | Gradually replaces the host's personality with a duller, crueler copy. Friends say "they changed." | Takes about a year to complete |
| **Nagaremono** (流れ者, the drifter) | Long-limbed grey figure walking behind the host | Fixation on another person; stalking behavior. Can jump from host to host along the line of obsession. | Dangerous to bystanders |
| **Ikiryō-dama** (生霊玉, grudge-knot) | A knot of the host's own face, floating near them | A fragment of the host's soul torn loose by a grudge, corrupted into a parasite. It attacks the person the host resents. | Kakuriyo calls these "living-ghost tumors" |
| **Mukuchi** (無口, the silencer) | A grey hand over the host's mouth | The host can't ask for help or talk about what's wrong | Often found together with Sabishi-mushi |
| **Ikari-ushi** (怒り牛, rage-ox) | A small horned bull-shape in the chest | Sudden violent rage; road-rage incidents, domestic violence | The Jingikan prioritizes these |

## 4. Tier 3: Magatsu (禍つ)

Free-roaming predator beasts that form in deep kegare pools, then **nest** in places of concentrated misery and hunt nearby. A Magatsu is the size of a car or bus and requires a full Harae-tai kumi or a strong yokai to destroy.

- **Nests:** each Magatsu lives in a nest, a place soaked in kegare. The nest is visible to seers as grey sludge on walls and floors, hanging grey threads and a smell of mildew and old blood. Ordinary people experience it as a "bad place."
- **Hunting:** Magatsu feed by draining emotion and kegare from people near their nest, amplifying despair. Strong Magatsu lure vulnerable people toward danger: the edge of a platform, a rooftop, an empty road at night.
- **Physical effect:** at full effort, a Magatsu can break glass, bend metal, cause electrical failures and push a person.

| Name | Appearance | Nest | Behavior | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hōmu-gui** (ホーム喰い, platform-eater) | A long grey eel-like body, as long as a train car, with a mouth of human teeth | Railway platforms and tracks | Coils beneath the platform edge and whispers to exhausted commuters to step closer. Feeds on despair. | Station bells; salt lines along the platform edge |
| **Zangyōbashira** (残業柱, overtime pillar) | A thick grey pillar of melted office chairs and paper, floor to ceiling, with eyes | Office floors after 10 p.m. | Pins workers at their desks with an invisible weight; feeds on exhaustion. Can collapse a person. | Breaking the routine: turning off all the lights, everyone leaving |
| **Haikyo-nushi** (廃墟主, ruin-lord) | A crawling mass of dust, broken furniture and old photographs | Abandoned buildings, closed hotels, empty schools | Territorial; attacks trespassers; collects the lost memories of those who lived there | Fire; returning a lost photograph to its owner weakens it |
| **Danchi-mukade** (団地百足, housing-block centipede) | A centipede 30 meters long with a segment for each empty apartment it has passed through | Aging public housing complexes | Threads through empty units through the walls; feeds on isolation; incubates Sabishi-mushi | Occupied, lit apartments block its path |
| **Kagami-gui** (鏡喰い, mirror-eater) | Visible only in mirrors: a second, distorted reflection | Bathrooms, changing rooms, gyms | Feeds on self-hatred; whispers about the victim's body | Covering mirrors; sincere compliments |
| **Yami-kaze** (闇風, dark wind) | A grey wind carrying faces | Intersections, highways at night | Causes accidents by blinding drivers for a moment | Peach-wood charms on the dashboard |
| **Nemuri-ba** (眠り場, the sleep-place) | A soft grey mass like a fog-filled futon | Internet cafés, capsule hotels, 24-hour fast-food restaurants | Feeds on people with nowhere to go; makes them unable to leave | Kindness from a stranger breaks its hold |
| **Kuragari-ko** (暗がり子, the dark-child) | A small grey figure at the edge of vision | Under staircases, in parking garages | Follows lone people; feeds on fear; harmless until it has grown | Saying out loud "I see you" makes it flee |

## 5. Tier 4: Ōmagatsu (大禍つ)

Calamity-class beings formed from the kegare of whole districts. There are four known Ōmagatsu in Tokyo in 2026. Each is a story arc, and each would require several Harae-tai companies, a Kishin, or the MC's household to destroy.

### Shūden (終電, "Last Train")
- **Nest:** the Shinjuku Station underground (the Labyrinth), extending through the Tokyo rail network after midnight.
- **Form:** an endless grey train with no driver, running on tracks that exist only in the Ura. Its cars are packed with thousands of sleeping grey figures with the faces of exhausted commuters. When it arrives, the doors open and a station chime plays.
- **Behavior:** feeds on the exhaustion of three million commuters a day. It lures the most exhausted toward platform edges, and when it is active, delays "due to personal injury" rise across the network. It can **take passengers**: a person who boards it falls asleep and does not wake, and their body is found sitting on a bench at a terminus. The Jingikan has recorded 41 "Shūden sleepers" since 2015.
- **Weakness:** the first train of the morning. Its power is lowest between 5 and 6 a.m. Station bells, salt on the rails.
- **Stance:** the Jingikan has failed to destroy it three times.

### Kurogane (黒鉄, "Black Iron")
- **Nest:** a 42-story office tower in Nishi-Shinjuku, headquarters of **Kurogane Holdings**, a mid-sized staffing and consulting company.
- **Form:** the building itself. Its upper floors are filled with grey Shigarami vines; its elevators are its mouths. Seen from the Ura, the tower looks like a black iron pillar oozing grey.
- **Behavior:** the Ōmagatsu has effectively **become a company**. Over twelve years, its Tsukimono have taken over the executives. Kurogane Holdings has 3,000 employees, a 140-hour monthly overtime culture, and a turnover rate of 60%. It contracts workers out to other companies, spreading Shigarami across Tokyo's offices. Its CEO, **Saeki Tōru**, is a Kagebōshi host who has been fully replaced.
- **Weakness:** the company is legally real. Destroying the Ōmagatsu without destroying the company does nothing, because the company will regrow it. It must be fought both spiritually and legally (labor inspection, exposure, a mass walkout).
- **Secret:** Kurogane sells its harvested kegare to the Jingikan's Rites Bureau, which sells it on to the Inari Group (see 13).

### Enjō (炎上, "Flaming")
- **Nest:** the internet, centered on servers and phones in the Ura.
- **Form:** a burning eye made of thousands of screens, visible to seers floating in the sky above Shibuya when it is active. Its body is a swarm of Kuchi-mushi and Netami-mushi riding a million phones.
- **Behavior:** coordinates online pile-ons. It picks a target (a person who said something clumsy, a small shop, a minor celebrity) and drives thousands of people to attack them. It feeds on the rage of the attackers and the despair of the target, and its victims often become Kodoku.
- **Weakness:** it cannot cross into Kakuriyo's Kumo-net, which runs on silk instead of electricity. If the boundary opens, the jorōgumo fear it will learn to.
- **Weak point:** its center moves between whatever devices are hosting its attention. It can be fought by an MC who finds the target and protects them.

### The Unclaimed (引取人なき, Hikitorinin-naki)
- **Class:** Apex, Kodoku line (see below).

## 6. Apex: The Kodoku Line (孤毒)

**Kodoku** is a pun. 孤独 (*kodoku*) means loneliness. 蠱毒 (*kodoku*) is an ancient curse ritual in which venomous creatures are sealed in a jar to devour each other until one remains, whose poison is then used to curse.

Kodoku are a separate lineage of Magatsu formed from **severed bonds** rather than general kegare. They are smarter, more human and more dangerous than other Magatsu.

### How Kodoku Form
- From **Sabishi-mushi** (loneliness glowworms) that grow fat on an isolated host for years.
- From people who die alone and unmissed (**kodokushi**, "lonely death," a real Japanese social problem in which people die and are not found for weeks or months).
- From the **frayed threads** of the 1.2 million bonds cut to make the Seal. Some of these never wove cleanly and have curdled.
- From a **lapsed ledger thread** when one of the MC's destined partners dies at the end of the 49-day clock (see 09).

### What Kodoku Are
- **Form:** a figure made of snarled grey thread, wet paper and loose pieces of faces. They are roughly human-sized, and often wear the face and clothes of the person whose loneliness made them.
- **Speech:** Kodoku can speak, usually in fragments of the person they formed from: "Is anyone there?" "Did you get my message?" "I'll be fine on my own."
- **Attacks:**
  - **Bond-drain:** wrapping a victim in grey thread drains their bonds. The victim forgets friends, partners stop recognizing each other, and threads fade to grey.
  - **Smothering:** physically wrapping and strangling.
  - **Isolation field:** within about 20 meters, phones lose signal, voices don't carry, and people stop noticing each other.
- **Why they hunt the MC:** Kodoku crave bonds. The MC is the largest knot of fate in either world. Kodoku are drawn to the MC from across a district, like moths to a lamp. When the MC fights in Utsushiyo, Kodoku come.
- **Redemption:** Kodoku can be killed (salt, peach wood and fire work), but they can also be **re-tied**. The MC, at 54 knots (see 12), can give a Kodoku a bond (friendship, family, belonging, not romance) and release it. A re-tied Kodoku born from a lonely death is laid to rest. A re-tied Kodoku born from frayed threads can sometimes become a new being, a small spirit or a person.

### Kodoku Tiers

| Stage | Name | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Larva | **Sabishi-mushi** | Pale glowworm on an isolated host's heart (see Tier 1) |
| Juvenile | **Hitori** (独り, "alone") | Child-sized grey figure; haunts a single apartment. Usually formed from a single lonely death. |
| Adult | **Kodoku** | Human-sized, wearing a face; mobile; hunts bonds |
| Elder | **Mukuro-tsumugi** (骸紡ぎ, corpse-spinner) | Large Kodoku that weaves other Kodoku together; can raise Hitori from Sabishi-mushi |
| Apex | **Ōkodoku**: The Unclaimed | See below |

### The Unclaimed (Hikitorinin-naki)
- **Origin:** "Hikitorinin no nai itai" is the Japanese legal term for **unclaimed remains**: the bodies of people no family member comes to collect. Tokyo processes thousands every year. The Unclaimed formed from them.
- **Form:** a **gashadokuro**, the giant skeleton of folklore made of the bones of the forgotten dead, 15 meters tall, made of grey thread wound over thousands of small bones. Its skull is a mosaic of faces. It walks through the Ura of Tokyo's aging housing complexes at night. Each step makes a sound like a phone ringing unanswered.
- **Behavior:** it herds Kodoku and raises new ones. It is the mother of the Kodoku line in Tokyo. Its nest is Kōyōdai Danchi in Hachiōji, two kilometers from Itokake Shrine, which is not a coincidence: it was drawn to Tsumugi's fading shrine for decades, waiting for her to die.
- **Goal:** it wants the MC. It does not want to kill the MC. It wants to **become the MC's partner** by consuming the MC's threads. It speaks with thousands of voices: "We have no one. We have no one. Hold us."
- **Defeating it:** it cannot be killed by force alone; it regrows from the forgotten dead. It can be laid to rest only by a mass re-tying: the MC giving a bond to every soul it is made from. This is a major arc, possible only after 54 knots.

## 7. Yomi's Forces

The Magatsu are animated by Yomi's pull, but they are not Yomi's soldiers. They are a side effect. As the Chibiki-iwa weakens, Yomi itself is sending agents up. Full details are in chapter 13.

- **Yomotsu-shikome (黄泉醜女, hags of Yomi):** tall, emaciated women with grey skin, long black hair and black teeth, who move impossibly fast. In myth they chased Izanagi. They herd Magatsu, collect souls stuck in transit, and scout. Since 2024 they have been sighted in Tokyo's subway tunnels. They are intelligent and speak in an old, formal Japanese. Each has the power of an Ōyōkai. They cannot bear peaches and stop to devour any food thrown at them, as in the myth when Izanagi threw down grapes and bamboo shoots.
- **Yomotsu-ikusa (黄泉軍, armies of Yomi):** the 1,500 dead soldiers of the myth. Armored grey skeletons with rotting banners. Not yet seen above ground.
- **Yakusa-no-Ikazuchi (八雷神, the Eight Thunders):** eight thunder gods born from Izanami's rotting body. Yomi's generals. One has been sighted in Utsushiyo (see 13).
- **Izanami:** asleep beyond the boulder, waiting for the Wager to turn.
