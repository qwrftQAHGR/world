# 06. Hyakki (百鬼): The Capital

## 1. Overview

- **Name:** Hyakki ("Hundred Demons"), named after the Hyakki Yagyō, the Night Parade of a Hundred Demons. Before 1917 it was called **Edo-ura**.
- **Location:** Musashi province, on the mirror-site of Tokyo.
- **Population:** 9.2 million in the metropolitan area; 4.1 million in the 23 central wards.
- **Composition:** 60% kemonobito, 27% yokai, 9% ijin, 4% Kakushi and others.
- **Look:** a dense modern city of towers and low tiled neighborhoods. Paper lanterns hang alongside neon signs in every shopping street. Silk Kumo-net lines glint overhead between buildings. Tengu and harpies fly between rooftops, ittan-momen couriers drift above traffic, and oboroguruma taxis rattle through the streets.
- **The night:** Hyakki is busiest at night. Many yokai are nocturnal, so shops, offices and trains run on two shifts, and the city's peak hour is around 9 p.m.
- **Climate:** the same as Tokyo's, with denser fog in winter and stronger festival-season humidity.

## 2. The Wards

| Ward | Utsushiyo equivalent | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Gijidō-ku** (Diet Ward) | Chiyoda | Government. The Gijidō (Parade Diet building), ministries, the Kebiishi-chō headquarters, embassies. At its center is the **Lantern Palace**, the former Kurama Shogunate castle, now a public park and museum. |
| **Kitsune-no-uchi** (Within the Fox) | Marunouchi, Ōtemachi | Finance. Inari Bank headquarters, the Inari Group's Hyakki tower, corporate offices. Vermilion torii gates line the main avenue for two kilometers. |
| **Kappabashi** (Kappa Bridge) | Asakusa, Taitō | Technology and manufacturing. Kawataro Industries' headquarters, workshops, electronics markets, kappa medicine shops. Canals run through the district, and kappa commute by water. |
| **Ōmagatoki-chō** (Twilight Town) | Shinjuku, Kabukichō | Nightlife and entertainment. Theaters, tanuki rakugo halls, bars, host and hostess clubs, kabuki, pachinko, idol venues. The lighting is permanently set to twilight colors. Bunbuku Group's Hyakki tower. |
| **Sumida-ura** | Sumida | The **Ōchōchin** (Great Lantern) tower, a 634-meter broadcasting tower shaped like a stack of paper lanterns that glows orange at night. The city's main landmark. |
| **Nekomachi** (Cat Town) | Yanaka, Nezu | An old low-rise neighborhood of narrow lanes, temples and cafés. Home of Hyakki's nekomata and cat-folk. Streamers' studios are tucked into old houses. |
| **Ueno-Kakushi** | Ueno | The **Kakushi Quarter**, Hyakki's human neighborhood: about 180,000 humans. Brokers of Utsushiyo goods, translators, human-food restaurants, a large shrine to the spirited-away. Hyakki's main zoo (whose exhibits include ordinary animals that do not talk). |
| **Shin-Dejima** | Odaiba, Tokyo Bay | An artificial island built in 1925 for Hyakki's foreign monster population: 380,000 ijin. Lamia trattorias, harpy rookeries, centaur stables converted to apartments, slime bathhouses, dwarven breweries, a Hōrai-town. |
| **Ōe-gashi** (Ōe Wharf) | Shinagawa, port area | Docks, shipyards, warehouses. Oni and minotaur dockworkers, Ōe Federation union halls, oni bars, the Shuten-kai's territory. |
| **Yume-no-shima** (Dream Island) | Yumenoshima, Kōtō | An island in the bay given over to the Baku Medical Network: dream clinics, sleep hospitals, a nightmare-quarantine ward. |
| **Kumo-machi** (Spider Town) | Akihabara | Jōren Weaveworks' Hyakki hub. Silk lines converge here in thick bundles. Electronics and silk-tech markets, gaming arcades, maid cafés staffed by jorōgumo. |
| **Kuwa-no-miyako** (Mulberry Capital) | Hachiōji | A western ward on the city's edge, Hyakki's old silk-weaving district. Contains **Itokake-chō** (see below). |
| **Takao** | Mount Takao area | The **Takao Garrison**, the tengu military base guarding western Hyakki, on the mountain's slopes. |
| **Shibaura-ura** | Minato | The Ryūkyū embassy, the Hōrai embassy, Kamuy Mosir's liaison office, luxury residences. |
| **Mitaka-no-mori** | Mitaka, Musashino | Forested western suburbs. Older yokai Houses' estates sit among the woods. |

## 3. Itokake-chō (糸掛町)

The neighborhood around the mirror-site of Itokake Shrine, in Kuwa-no-miyako ward.

- **Layout:** a hillside of narrow lanes lined with old two-story wooden weaving houses with lattice windows. Silk threads hang drying across the lanes. The rhythm of looms can be heard from most houses.
- **Population:** about 3,000, mostly **jorōgumo** weavers and their families, plus kemonobito silk workers, a few tsukumogami looms and spinning wheels, and a small community of silk-moth folk (kemonobito with feathery antennae and soft wings).
- **The shrine:** **Itokake Shrine (Kakuriyo)**. Unlike its Utsushiyo twin, it was never closed. When Tsumugi lost her spindle in 1909 and fell asleep, the jorōgumo of Itokake-chō kept her shrine in perfect order for 117 years without a god inside: sweeping, offering, repairing. The keeper is **Ayame** (see 14). The shrine has a wooden torii, a main hall, a storehouse (the Kakuriyo entrance to the Mayoiga), a mulberry tree, and a **chichi-ichō** ginkgo that mirrors the one in Hachiōji.
- **The Weavers' Guild (Itokake-za):** a guild of 140 jorōgumo master weavers that controls the neighborhood. It is affiliated with, but independent of, Jōren Weaveworks. The guild still keeps the old shrine festival every November.
- **Reaction to the MC:** when Tsumugi wakes, the whole neighborhood feels it: every loom in Itokake-chō stops for a moment, and the shrine bells ring by themselves. The MC arriving in Itokake-chō is a homecoming the neighborhood has waited a century for.

## 4. Transit

- **Oboro Line**: the loop line around central Hyakki, the equivalent of the Yamanote Line. 29 stations. Painted lantern-orange. Runs 24 hours.
- **Hyakki Metro**: 13 subway lines. The deepest stations are lit by foxfire.
- **Hayate** bullet trains depart from **Hyakki Central Station** (in Kitsune-no-uchi) for Heian-kyō (1 hour 50 minutes), Izumo (4 hours 30 minutes) and the north.
- **Trams** in the old neighborhoods.
- **Canals** in Kappabashi, used by kappa commuters and water taxis.
- **Oboroguruma taxis**: flag one down by raising a lantern. Base fare 50 mon.
- **Ittan-momen couriers**: flying cloth riders, the fastest way to send a package.
- **Airship port**: **Haneda-ura**, in the bay. Kurama Air flights to all provinces.

## 5. Landmarks

- **The Gijidō**: the Diet building. Black timber, six stories, with a roof of copper tiles and a great lantern above the entrance that is lit when the Diet is in session.
- **The Lantern Palace**: the old shogunate castle. A public park with a museum of the Kurama Shogunate and the Lantern Restoration.
- **Ōchōchin Tower**: the 634-meter Great Lantern tower.
- **The Thousand Torii of Kitsune-no-uchi**: 1,000 vermilion torii over the main avenue of the financial district, each donated by a company.
- **Hyakki Central Station**: a red-brick-and-tile station modeled on Tokyo Station, but twice the size.
- **The Ōe Union Hall**: a massive concrete hall in Ōe-gashi, headquarters of the Ōe Federation of Labor.
- **The Kawa-zumō Kokugikan**: the national kappa sumo arena, with a water-filled ring.
- **Shin-Dejima Bridge**: the bridge linking Shin-Dejima to the mainland, lined with the flags of the foreign communities.
- **The Shrine of the Spirited-Away**: in Ueno-Kakushi, a shrine where the names of all known spirited-away humans are written on wooden plaques. About 200,000 plaques.
- **The Night Parade Route**: the route of the annual Hyakki Yagyō Festival (see 15), marked by stone lanterns through six wards.

## 6. Life in Hyakki: Practical Details

- **Phone:** KD phone with Kumo-net service, about 4 ryō a month.
- **Rent:** one room in a central ward costs 80–120 ryō a month; a family apartment in the suburbs costs 150 ryō.
- **Konbini:** an Inari Mart or Pon Mart on nearly every corner.
- **Crime:** pickpocketing by kamaitachi, tanuki con artists, Shuten-kai protection rackets in Ōe-gashi, and illegal foxfire gambling in Ōmagatoki-chō.
- **Police boxes (kōban)** staffed by karasu-tengu stand at major intersections.
- **Magatsu:** visible pests. Mushi are netted out of windows. Larger Magatsu are rare inside the city and are culled by tengu rangers at the outskirts. Nests in abandoned buildings are reported to the Kebiishi-chō pest division.
