# 03. Utsushiyo: The Hidden Side of Human Japan

To nearly everyone in it, Utsushiyo is ordinary modern Japan. Its hidden population, roughly:

| Group | Number | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Mushi (spirit-bugs) | Hundreds of millions | Everywhere |
| Larger Magatsu | Tens of thousands; rising | Concentrated in cities |
| Nokori (stranded yokai) | About 30,000 | 22,000 registered, 8,000 unregistered |
| Yadonashi-gami (shrineless gods) | About 2,400 | Illegal |
| Enshrined (licensed) kami | About 9,000 active | On the Faith Grid |
| Toshi-densetsu (urban-legend yokai) | About 200 major, thousands minor | Tolerated |
| Mieru-hito (born seers) | About 12,000 | Mostly hiding; some recruited |
| Jingikan personnel | About 4,800 | Secret state office |
| Onmyō House practitioners | About 600 | Private contractors |
| Yūrei (ghosts) | Uncounted; rising | Everywhere |

## 1. The Jingikan (神祇官)

### Overview
The **Jingikan** (Department of Divinities) is a secret Japanese government office responsible for everything spiritual in Utsushiyo. It was officially abolished in 1871 and has operated in secret ever since. It created and maintains the Seal between the worlds, maintains the Veil, runs the Faith Grid, licenses gods and exorcists, registers stranded yokai and fights the Magatsu infestation.

- **Cover identity:** the **National Folklore Preservation Office** (Kokumin Minzoku Hozon-kyoku), a small, dull-looking office that officially catalogs festivals and folk customs. Its budget is hidden inside the Agency for Cultural Affairs. Fewer than thirty elected officials know what it really is.
- **Headquarters:** **Sōmusubi Jingū**, central Tokyo (see Locations). The operational offices are in **Hall 7**, a plain concrete annex behind the shrine's treasure house.
- **Personnel:** about 4,800, including priests, soldiers, researchers, clerks and memory-cleanup crews.
- **Budget:** officially ¥400 million a year. Actually about ¥38 billion, plus the stored Shinkō of the Faith Grid.
- **Culture:** a tired, old-fashioned government bureaucracy that is overworked and underfunded for its real job. Its field personnel are mostly decent people doing a hard job, and many are burned out. Its leadership believes, sincerely, that the Seal is what keeps modern Japan intact.

### Leadership
- **Jingi-haku (Director): Hakuō Tadanori**, 71. Descended from the family that held the Jingi-haku title in the imperial court for seven centuries. Tall, thin, white-haired, soft-spoken; always wears a dark suit with a small silver crest of interlocking rings. He believes opening the worlds would kill millions through a reiatsu flood and yokai predation, and that the Seal is a tragic necessity. He knows about the Inari kegare arrangement (see 13) and considers it an acceptable cost. He knows the infestation was caused by the consolidation and has kept it secret for forty years.
- **Deputy Director (Field): Kurokawa Isao**, 54. Former Harae-tai commander, pragmatic and exhausted. He thinks the Seal is failing and that the Jingikan needs a new plan. A possible ally for the MC.
- **Head of the Rites Bureau: Mibu Sachiko**, 62. Controls the Faith Grid. Cold, precise and politically ambitious. She is the Jingikan's main contact with the Inari Group.

### Divisions

**1. Harae-tai (祓隊, Purification Squads).** Field extermination units.
- About 1,500 operators nationwide; about 400 in Tokyo, organized in eight companies.
- Basic unit: a **kumi** of four: one barrier-caster (kekkai-shi), two gunners, one seer-spotter (usually a recruited Mieru-hito).
- Wear dark navy tactical uniforms with white cord at the shoulder. In public they pass as utility or gas company workers.
- Morale is low and turnover is high. Average operator kegare is 35 (Smudged), and many are Stained.
- Since about 2018 the Harae-tai has quietly paid shrineless gods through intermediaries to clear nests it cannot handle.

**2. Kekkai-ryō (結界寮, Boundary Office).** Maintains the Seal and the Veil.
- **Seal Section:** monitors the Shimenawa through instruments in the Sōmusubi vault; performs the Ōmisoka rite each New Year's Eve to reinforce it.
- **Veil Section:** performs the rites at the great shrines that keep the Veil working.
- **Cleanup crews (Kakushi-gumi):** respond after incidents. They plant explanations ("gas leak," "electrical fire"), adjust news stories through contacts in the media, and use a technique called **Wasure-fuda** (forgetting talismans) on witnesses who saw too much. A Wasure-fuda erases about an hour of memory and produces 5 kegare in the target.
- **Field observers:** assigned to watch spiritually anomalous people. **Kirishima Aoi** is a Kekkai-ryō field observer (see 14).

**3. Saishi-kyoku (祭祀局, Rites Bureau).** Runs the Faith Grid.
- Manages the stored Shinkō of the major state-registered shrines.
- Keeps the **Enshrined Registry**, the list of licensed kami. Licensed kami receive a stipend of Shinkō from the grid in exchange for staying in their shrines and obeying the Jingikan. About 9,000 kami are registered.
- Secretly sells refined kegare to Kakuriyo through the Inari Group (see 13).

**4. Kanbe Academy (神戸学院).** Training school for recruited Mieru-hito and the children of Jingikan families. Located in a former girls' school in Kamakura. About 300 students, aged 16–22. Graduates enter the Harae-tai or Kekkai-ryō.

**5. Jūmin-ka (住民課, Resident Affairs Section).** Keeps the **Resident Spirit Registry** of Nokori yokai and toshi-densetsu. Registered spirits get a card, must report address changes, and must not use powers in public. Unregistered spirits are hunted.

**6. Hasami-gumi (鋏組, Scissors Unit).** A small secret unit of about forty that cuts cross-realm threads that slip past the Seal, usually after Tanabata or an accidental crossing. Trained in severance techniques by priests of the goddess Tachikiri. In 2019 its captain, **Kiriya Sōgen**, and eleven members defected to found the **Kirigami-shū** (see 13). The rest of the unit still serves, and is now assigned to the MC.

**7. Archive (書庫).** The deepest records: the Survey of Crossings, the weave diagrams of the Seal, the true history. Located in the vault level beneath Sōmusubi Jingū.

### Law
The secret **Spirit Affairs Ordinance** of 1915 governs spiritual matters. It is enforced by Jingikan tribunals, which are closed hearings without lawyers.
- Unlicensed spiritual practice: fines, sealing of powers, or memory sealing.
- Unregistered spirit residence: deportation to Kakuriyo through the Kanmon (rare), sealing in a talisman, or destruction.
- Unauthorized crossing: detention.
- Severe offenses: imprisonment at **Sekisho Prison**, a facility built into a mountain in Shiga Prefecture, where prisoners' powers are sealed by a permanent kekkai.

### Stance Toward the MC
- **Initially:** surveillance. A field observer (Kirishima Aoi) is assigned.
- **After the first knots:** containment. The Hasami-gumi is tasked with cutting the MC's threads, and the Harae-tai with bringing the MC in.
- **Internally divided:** Hakuō wants the MC contained, by sealing or imprisonment if necessary. Kurokawa wants to talk. Mibu wants to control the MC as a new source of power.

## 2. The Onmyō Houses (陰陽家)

Four families descended from the old court diviners. They work as **private contractors**, exorcising for corporations, politicians and wealthy families who can pay. They are licensed by the Jingikan and draw on the Faith Grid, but they keep their own stored Shinkō as well and resent Jingikan control.

| House | Base | Specialty | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Abe-ryū** | Kyoto | Classical onmyōdō: shikigami, Seiman star seals, divination | The oldest and most prestigious. Claims descent from Abe no Seimei, whose mother was said to be the fox **Kuzunoha**, so the house carries fox blood. Elitist, formal and expensive. Head: **Abe no Haruaki**, 66. Heir: **Abe no Seika**, 25 (see 14). |
| **Ashiya-ryū** | Harima (Hyōgo) | Combat onmyōdō: Dōman grid, curses, counter-curses | Descended from the followers of Ashiya Dōman, Seimei's rival. Rough and mercenary; will take any job, including curses. Head: **Ashiya Gonzō**, 58. |
| **Kurozumi-ryū** | Tokyo (Roppongi) | Tech onmyōdō: drones, seal apps, data divination | New money, founded in 1962. Runs a consultancy that sells "spiritual risk audits" to corporations. Head: **Kurozumi Reiko**, 44. Their tech prodigy **Kurozumi Yū** is a ledger partner (see 14). |
| **Hanayagi-ryū** | Nara | Kagura dance, bells, oracle possession | A miko lineage led by women, with dragon blood. Works mostly with shrines. The most respected of the four by kami. Head: **Hanayagi Suzune**, 70. Heir: **Hanayagi Shizuru**, 24 (see 14). |

**Stance toward the MC:** each House wants to secure the MC for itself. Abe-ryū wants the MC under its protection as a source of prestige. Ashiya-ryū will accept a contract to capture the MC. Kurozumi-ryū wants data. Hanayagi-ryū consults its oracle and finds the MC's threads written in it.

## 3. The Yadonashi-gami (宿無し神): Shrineless Gods

### Who They Are
When the Shrine Consolidation closed 70,000 shrines, most of their gods did not die. About 2,400 survive in 2026, drifting through Japan's cities without a home, almost without worshippers, and slowly fading. About 300 live in Tokyo.

- **Appearance:** a shrineless god looks like a human (or sometimes an animal) with something slightly off: eyes too bright, a faint glow in the dark, no shadow at noon. Most dress in cheap secondhand clothes. They are invisible to ordinary people unless they choose to be seen, and even then people tend to forget them.
- **Power:** low. A Yadonashi-gami typically has the power of a weak Yōkai, with flashes of their old strength when sincerely prayed to.
- **Fading:** a shrineless god who receives no sincere prayer for about a year begins to fade. They go translucent, lose memories, and eventually vanish. About 30 disappear each year.

### How They Survive
**Freelance work.** They answer prayers no one else is listening to: wishes scrawled on bathroom walls, prayers whispered at dead roadside shrines, a drunk begging the sky. They clear Mushi from people, break up small Magatsu nests, find lost things, and nudge luck.

**Payment: the 5-yen coin.** In Japanese, *go-en* (five yen) sounds exactly like *go-en* (ご縁, a bond). The 5-yen coin is the traditional shrine offering for this reason. A 5-yen coin given sincerely is the only money that carries **En**, and to a shrineless god it is food. A single sincerely offered 5-yen coin feeds a Yadonashi-gami for about a day.

**Kamidanomi (神頼み).** Since 2023, the tanuki-owned Bunbuku Group has run **Kamidanomi**, a gig app. Humans experience it as a "prayer journal" wellness app that lets them write down wishes. Gods with the god-side version of the app see the wishes as jobs, accept them, and are paid in **digital offerings** that Bunbuku converts to Shinkō, minus a 30% commission. Most shrineless gods hate the app and use it anyway. It has about 1.4 million human users.

**Legal status:** under the Ordinance, shrineless gods are unlicensed spirits. The Jingikan has offered them registration on the condition that they be enshrined as subordinate gods in the great shrines, which means losing their names and their independence. Most refuse.

### The Kamidomari (神泊まり)
A cheap lodging house (**doya**) in **Sanya**, Tokyo's old day-laborer district in Taitō ward. It looks like every other run-down doya on the street: a narrow four-story concrete building with tiny rooms rented by the night. Its rooms are rented to gods. About sixty shrineless gods live there at any time.

- **Manager: Funado**, the god of boundaries and crossroads (see 08).
- **Ground floor:** a common room with a television, a rice cooker, a shrine shelf where residents leave each other 5-yen coins, and a bulletin board of job postings.
- **Rules:** no fighting inside, rent paid in 5-yen coins (one per night) or in work, and any god who has not been prayed to in a month gets fed by the others.
- **Atmosphere:** shabby, warm and funny. Arguments over the television remote. A rice-planting goddess cooking curry. An old war god teaching younger gods to fight in the alley.

### Notable Shrineless Gods
- **Funado** (see 08). Manager of the Kamidomari, ancient boundary god.
- **Satsuki** (ledger partner, see 14). Rice-planting goddess, works at a standing soba shop.
- **Mikuji** (ledger partner, see 14). God of fortune slips, a gambler.
- **Furu-ido-sama** (ledger partner, see 14). God of an old well, quiet, a healer.
- **Ōtoshi-dono**, a harvest god in his eighties who runs the alley dojo and teaches fighting. Not a ledger partner.
- **Kamado-no-Ochiba**, a hearth goddess of a demolished farmhouse shrine. She cooks for the Kamidomari. Not a ledger partner.
- **The Seven Small Ones (Nanakami)**, seven tiny gods of a demolished roadside shrine, each the size of a sparrow. They work together as messengers and spies.

### Stance Toward the MC
Each knot the MC ties to a shrineless god gives that god a permanent source of En, ending their fading. The MC's restoration of Itokake Shrine shows that a closed shrine can be reopened. To the Yadonashi-gami, the MC is the first hope in a century.

## 4. Mieru-hito (見える人): The Ones Who See

About one human in 10,000 is born with spiritual sight, roughly 12,000 people in Japan.

- **Life as a seer:** seeing a Magatsu means it notices you. Seers learn young to look away, pretend not to see, and never react. Many are diagnosed with anxiety disorders or psychosis. Many have Mushi and Tsukimono drawn to them. A significant number break down.
- **Recruitment:** the Jingikan identifies seers through school counselors and psychiatric referrals and recruits them to Kanbe Academy, usually at 16–18.
- **The Mienai-kai (見えない会, "Don't-See Society"):** an anonymous online forum and support network for seers who refuse recruitment. Members share tips (which streets to avoid, how to look away convincingly, which shrines still work), run three safehouses in Tokyo, and warn each other about the Jingikan.
- **The Shioya (塩屋, "Salt-House"):** a vigilante group of about twenty seers in western Tokyo, based in Hachiōji, who fight Mushi and small Magatsu with salt, prayer beads, peach-wood weapons and baseball bats. Their leader is **Ōtsuki Ran** (see 14). They operate out of the back room of a closed-down batting cage called **Swing Hachiōji**.

## 5. The Nokori (残り): Stranded Yokai

When the Seal closed in 1914, about 90,000 yokai were in Utsushiyo. About 30,000 survive in 2026.

- **Life:** most live as humans, in human form, in human jobs: a fox running a tofu shop, a kappa working at a public bath, a tanuki selling pottery. Many married humans. Their descendants are the source of most **hidden heritage** in Utsushiyo's population.
- **Weakness:** in Utsushiyo's thin reiatsu, they operate at 50% power, and most have spent a century without real Osore or Shinkō. Many are physically weak, low in rank, and aging faster than they should.
- **Registration:** about 22,000 are registered with the Jingikan's Resident Affairs Section. They carry registration cards, report address changes, and are forbidden from using powers in public. About 8,000 are unregistered and live in hiding.
- **Ura-Asakusa (裏浅草):** a hidden market in the alleys behind Asakusa, Tokyo, in the Ura layer. Visible only to those with sight. Nokori run shops here: kappa medicines, fox-made aburaage, tanuki-forged talismans, smuggled Kakuriyo goods (via Mujina-michi tunnels), secondhand spiritual weapons, and information. It operates from dusk to dawn. The Jingikan knows about it and tolerates it as a pressure valve.
- **Notable Nokori:** **Hoshino Fuki**, 89, a kitsune who runs a tofu shop in Hachiōji. She is the grandmother of ledger partner **Hoshino Kaito** and has never told him what she is.

## 6. Toshi-densetsu (都市伝説): Urban-Legend Yokai

Yokai born in the modern era from modern human fear: rumors, internet stories and panics. They are native to Utsushiyo and live in the Ura.

- **Power:** almost entirely Osore-fueled. Strong where their story is told, weak where it is forgotten.
- **The script:** each toshi-densetsu is partly bound to its legend. Kuchisake-onna feels a compulsion to ask "Am I pretty?" Many of them hate this.
- **Status:** the Jingikan tolerates them because they absorb fear that would otherwise feed Magatsu, and registers them with the Resident Affairs Section. Kakuriyo's old Houses consider them upstarts and vermin.
- **Kisaragi Station (きさらぎ駅):** their capital and stronghold. A phantom station on a single-track rural line that exists outside normal space, on the border of Utsushiyo, Kakuriyo and the edge of Yomi. Late-night trains on certain lines in Shizuoka and around Tokyo sometimes stop there. The station is a small, old wooden platform surrounded by dark fields and mountains, with a tunnel that leads somewhere else. Over twenty years the toshi-densetsu have built a town in the fields around it: shacks, a bar, a shrine to no god, and a market. About 400 toshi-densetsu live there. It is ruled by the station master, **Kisaragi Yoru** (see 14).
- **Notable toshi-densetsu:**
  - **Kuchisake-onna** (see 14, ledger partner, "Masako"). The Slit-Mouthed Woman, born 1979.
  - **Kisaragi Yoru** (see 14, ledger partner). The station master.
  - **Jinmenken.** Dogs with human faces that mutter insults. They are a pack of about thirty and work as Kisaragi Station's guards and messengers.
  - **Kunekune.** A white wavering figure seen in rice fields. Looking at it too long causes madness. A loner who lives in the fields around Kisaragi Station.
  - **The Red Room (Aka-i Heya).** A pop-up ad legend that has become a sentient room which appears inside internet cafés. Dangerous and not friendly.

## 7. Locations

### Itokake Shrine (糸掛神社)
- **Location:** a wooded hill in **Kuwagaoka** ("Mulberry Hill"), a fictional residential neighborhood in northern Hachiōji, western Tokyo. Hachiōji was historically Japan's "mulberry capital," a silk-weaving town at the head of the silk road to Yokohama.
- **Approach:** a stone staircase of 108 steps rises from a dead-end street between a parking lot and a closed-down kimono-fabric warehouse. Bamboo has grown over the stairs.
- **Grounds:** a small clearing at the top. A weathered stone torii with its name plaque cracked. One stone komainu (**Un-gyō**) on the left, mouth closed; an empty pedestal on the right where **A-gyō** stood. A small wooden main hall (honden) with a sagging roof. A storehouse (**kura**) with a heavy wooden door, which is the entrance to the Mayoiga. A dry purification basin. A large **mulberry tree** grown through the old offering hall. An ancient **ginkgo** with breast-shaped burls hanging from its branches (a **chichi-ichō**, traditionally prayed to for milk).
- **Legal status:** the land is officially owned by Hachiōji city, earmarked for a park that was never built.
- **After Tsumugi wakes:** the shrine slowly restores itself as the MC ties knots: bamboo recedes, the basin refills, the roof straightens. At 9 knots it looks cared-for. At 27 it looks newly built. Pilgrims begin to come.

### Sōmusubi Jingū (総結神宮)
- **Location:** central Tokyo, on a raised wooded site near the Imperial Palace moat. About 6 hectares.
- **Appearance:** a large, austere state shrine built in 1910 in unpainted cypress, with a gravel approach lined with stone lanterns, one from each of the 400 small shrines it absorbed. A popular, quiet place for tourists and wedding photos.
- **Underneath:** five basement levels cut into rock.
  - B1: Jingikan Archive.
  - B2: offices and holding cells.
  - B3: the **Kanmon**, the official crossing to Kakuriyo: a torii set into a stone wall, guarded at all times by Harae-tai.
  - B4: Seal monitoring instruments: a room of looms with thread connecting them to the walls.
  - B5: **The Itomaki Vault.** A circular stone chamber. In the center, Tsumugi's spindle stands upright, a meter tall, wound with a cord of grey and red threads as thick as a man's body, which runs up through the ceiling into nothing. Guarding it, bound by a vault seal that makes him obey the Jingi-haku: **A-gyō**, the missing komainu.

### Sanya (山谷)
Tokyo's old day-laborer district, north of Asakusa in Taitō ward. Cheap lodging houses (doya), aging former laborers, welfare offices, small bars. Home of the **Kamidomari**. Its high level of loneliness and kegare makes it a Magatsu breeding ground, which the gods of the Kamidomari keep in check.

### Kōyōdai Danchi (紅葉台団地)
A fictional public housing complex in Hachiōji built in 1971: twelve five-story concrete blocks on a hillside, with about 1,800 units. Since the 1990s its residents have aged and moved out; about 40% of units are empty. It has the highest rate of **kodokushi** (people dying alone and undiscovered) in western Tokyo. In 2026 it is a major **Magatsu nest** and the birthplace of many Kodoku (see 04). It is 2 kilometers from Itokake Shrine.

### Shinjuku Station
The world's busiest train station, with over 200 exits and a maze of underground passages. Its lowest levels, after the last train, are a large Magatsu nest called **the Labyrinth** by exterminators. It feeds on the exhaustion of 3 million daily commuters. The Ōmagatsu **Shūden** (see 04) is centered here.

### Mount Takao (高尾山)
A sacred mountain in western Hachiōji, 8 kilometers from Itokake Shrine, associated in real life with tengu. In Utsushiyo, a handful of Nokori tengu still live on its slopes disguised as hiking-trail volunteers. In Kakuriyo, its mirror is the site of the **Takao Garrison**, the tengu base nearest to the MC's mirror-shrine.

### Ura-Asakusa (裏浅草)
See Nokori above. The hidden market behind Asakusa, open dusk to dawn.

### Swing Hachiōji
A closed-down batting cage in Hachiōji, base of the Shioya vigilantes.
