# 09. The Threads and the Ledger

## 1. The Red Thread of Fate

In East Asian legend, an invisible red thread (**akai ito**) runs from the little finger of each person to the little finger of the person they are destined to be with. In this setting it is real.

- **Who spins them:** fate (the collective will of the gods, especially Ōkuninushi's office) spins threads between people at birth. Matchmaking gods like Tsumugi tend them.
- **How many:** most people are born with **one** red thread or none. A small number have two. Having none is not a tragedy: most people live happily with non-fated loves (pink threads), friendships (gold) and families (white).
- **What a red thread does:** it makes two people likely to meet, likely to recognize each other, and likely to be deeply happy together. It does not force love. It does not guarantee anything.
- **Strength:** a red thread is the strongest kind of bond. A tied red thread produces large amounts of En and makes both people healthier and luckier.
- **Visibility:** visible only to those with thread-sight (the MC, the MC's children, matchmaking gods, Hare Office messengers).
- **Cross-realm threads:** before 1914, about one person in five in the Musashi region had a red thread leading to someone in the other realm. These cross-realm bonds held the crossings open.

---

## 2. What the Seal Did to the Threads

- **1906–1914:** the Jingikan cut about **1.2 million** cross-realm red threads and wove them into the Shimenawa of Severance.
- **Since 1914:** every new cross-realm red thread is pulled into the Shimenawa at birth.
- **Effect on people:** a person whose cross-realm thread was pulled into the Seal lives with a lifelong feeling that something is missing. They meet people, fall in love and have families, but there is a hollow place. Many never marry. Some are deeply lonely. Sabishi-mushi are drawn to them.
- **Orphaned threads:** some threads in the weave are anchored at only one end. This happens when the partner on the other side died, was never born because of the Seal, or had their thread snap during the weaving. Their loose ends hang inside the Shimenawa. There are about **40,000** orphaned threads in the weave.

## 3. The Futa-musubi Rite: How the MC Became the Binder

### The Prayer
The MC climbs the 108 steps of Itokake Shrine and prays sincerely. *(The MC's circumstances and the content of the prayer are set in chapter 17.)* This is the first sincere prayer offered at the shrine since 1909.

### The Hooks of the Heart
In Buddhism, there are **108 earthly desires** (bonnō). In this setting, the human heart has **108 hooks**, one for each desire. Each hook can hold one thread. Ordinary people use one or two: a red thread on one hook, perhaps another bond on a second.

### The Rite
Tsumugi wakes from her 117-year sleep and finds the MC. She sees that:
- The MC was born with an orphaned thread of their own. Its far end leads into the Shimenawa and is lost there.
- The MC's 108 hooks are empty and unusually strong.

With the last of her power, she performs the **Futa-musubi** (二結び, "double tying"), an ancient rite of her shrine:
1. She **opens all 108 hooks** of the MC's heart.
2. She reaches into the Shimenawa through the old bond between her shrine and her spindle, and pulls out the loose ends of the **108 strongest orphaned threads** she can reach. Their holders are spread across both realms.
3. She ties each loose end to one of the MC's hooks. The MC becomes the new anchor for 108 people whose destined partners were taken from them.
4. To make the MC able to bear these bonds in every direction (with partners of every sex, giving and receiving), she makes the MC **futanari**: both halves of generation, Izanagi and Izanami in one body. *Musubi* means both "tying" and "creative, generative force," and the MC is made into its embodiment.
5. She gives the MC **thread-sight** and the power to tie knots.

The MC becomes the **Musubi-bito** (結び人, "the Binder").

### What the MC Feels
At the moment of the rite, the MC feels 108 threads snap tight from their left little finger, running in every direction: along the ground, into the sky, down into the earth, into the Shimenawa. They feel a pull from each one. They see, faintly, the outline of 108 strangers. This lasts a moment and then settles into a constant background awareness.

### The Ledger Skein
After the rite, Tsumugi spins a physical record: the **Hyakuhachi-ito** (百八糸, "108 threads"), a skein of 108 silk strands that hangs from the rafters of Itokake Shrine's main hall. Each strand shows the status of one ledger thread:

| Strand appearance | Status |
|---|---|
| Pale, almost white | Unmet |
| Red with a glowing ember at the tip | **Lit**: clock running. The ember burns down over 49 days. |
| Red with a slower, smaller ember | Provisionally knotted (kari-musubi): clock at half speed |
| Deep solid red, knotted | **Tied** |
| Grey, snapped | Lapsed: the partner died |
| Black-tipped | Cut by an enkiri blade |

## 4. The Ledger: Rules

### Who Is on It
- **108 people**, each holding one orphaned thread now tied to the MC.
- They are spread across both realms and every kind of person: humans, beastfolk, yokai, ijin, toshi-densetsu, tsukumogami and gods.
- They are of every sex and gender.
- **All are adults.** A thread cannot light, and its holder cannot be identified as a ledger partner, until its holder is an adult: 18 or older in human terms, and physically and mentally mature for their species. This is a law of the Futa-musubi rite, not a choice anyone makes.
- **Fixed number.** The ledger is closed at 108. No thread can be added, and none can be transferred.
- **No children of the MC are ever on the ledger.** The MC's children are bound to the MC by white family threads. Their own red threads always lead outward, to other people.
- **No one knows the full list at the start,** not even Tsumugi. The threads reveal themselves when the MC comes near.
- **Several known ledger partners** are seeded in chapter 14. The rest are open for the story to introduce.

### Unlit Threads
- Before a partner is met, their thread is **unlit**. The MC can see unlit threads faintly, as pale red lines running from their own little finger, within about **1 kilometer**.
- Threads leading to partners in the other realm run **down into the ground** and vanish into the Shimenawa. When the MC crosses into the other realm, those threads appear and the ones for the realm just left vanish.
- After 9 knots (Thread-pull), the MC can feel the **direction** of any unlit thread in the same realm at any distance, and can follow it.
- The holder of an unlit thread feels nothing unusual beyond the lifelong hollow feeling described above.

### Lighting
A thread **lights** when the MC and the holder are physically near each other (within about 10 meters) **and** the MC's thread-sight touches the thread. In practice this happens the first time they meet in person.

**What the MC feels when a thread lights:** a sharp tug at the little finger, heat running up the arm, and sudden clarity: the MC sees the red thread blaze between them and the partner, with a burning ember at the partner's end.

**What the partner feels:** a sudden tug at their own little finger, a jolt of recognition ("I know you," though they don't), a rush of warmth or alarm, and over the following days an ache when apart from the MC, vivid dreams of the MC, and the hollow feeling they've lived with all their life suddenly having a direction.

Partners do not automatically understand what is happening. Most need to be told, and many don't believe it.

### The 49-Day Clock
When a thread lights, a clock starts. The holder has **49 days** for the knot to be tied. The number comes from the 49 days a soul spends in transit after death in Buddhist tradition.

- The clock runs on **outside time** (the time of the realm the partner is in). Time spent in the Mayoiga's fast stream does not count, and also does not stop the clock (see 11).
- The clock is visible to the MC, Tsumugi and Inaba as the ember on the thread. Tsumugi can read it to the day.
- **There is no way to stop the clock** except tying the knot.
- **The partner's health declines toward the end.** From day 40 on, the partner has trouble sleeping, loses appetite and feels cold. From day 46 on, they are weak and bedridden.

### The Provisional Knot (Kari-musubi, 仮結び)
A slower path for partners who need time.
- **How:** the MC and the partner both sincerely acknowledge the bond (a confession of feeling, or a sincere promise to try) and exchange a token. The traditional token is a **red silk cord tied around each other's left little finger**. The cords are spun by Tsumugi or the Itokake-chō weavers.
- **Effect:** the clock runs at **half speed** from that moment. A partner who makes a provisional knot on day 1 has 98 days.
- **Limit:** only one provisional knot per thread. It cannot be renewed.
- **Breaking it:** if either side sincerely withdraws, the provisional knot breaks and the clock resumes at full speed.

### Tying the Knot
- **The Knot is tied by the first sincere sexual union between the MC and the partner.**
- **Requirements:**
  - **Mutual consent.** Both must want it.
  - **Sincerity (makoto).** Both must mean it. It does not require full romantic love; it requires real desire and real feeling toward each other. A union that is purely transactional or performed out of duty alone does not tie the knot. It gives at most a weak Grace (see 10) and the clock keeps running.
  - **Coercion breaks it.** If either party is coerced (by force, threat, blackmail, magic or drugs), the thread **snaps immediately**. The holder dies at the next midnight, and the coercer is cursed (see below).
- **At the moment of the Knot:** both feel the thread pull tight and lock. The ember goes out. The MC sees the thread change from burning red to deep solid red. The partner receives the **Knot blessing** (see 10). By tradition the partner tells the MC their true name (see 02), though this is not required.
- **Once tied, a knot is permanent** unless cut by an enkiri blade (see below).

### Lapse (Death)
If the 49 days (or 98 with a provisional knot) run out without a knot:
- At **midnight** on the last day, the thread snaps.
- The partner dies: their heart stops, usually quietly, in sleep.
- Their soul enters the 49-day transit.
- **Seven days later**, the snapped thread curdles into a **Thread-lapse Kodoku**: a Kodoku wearing the dead partner's face and clothes, carrying their memories in fragments, which knows the MC and hunts them (see 04).
- The ledger strand turns grey.
- **The ledger does not refill.** The total of possible knots drops below 108 (see the Bridge, below).
- **The MC can lay a Thread-lapse Kodoku to rest** by re-tying it (possible at 54 knots), which releases the partner's soul to pass on. **It does not bring them back.**

### Refusal
A partner can refuse the MC, and the MC cannot force them. A partner who refuses dies when the clock runs out. This is the central tragedy of the ledger. Partners who refuse may change their minds before the end.

**Why a partner might refuse:** they're already in love with someone else; they don't believe it; they're afraid of the MC, or of the other 107; they're opposed on principle; they're from a faction hostile to the MC; they have reasons of their own.

**Existing relationships:** a partner who already has a spouse or lover is not required to leave them. The Knot adds a bond; it does not cut others. In Kakuriyo, where multiple marriage is legal, this is manageable. In Utsushiyo it is often painful.

## 5. Sibling Threads (Kyōdai-ito, 兄弟糸)

When a partner is knotted, a faint thread forms between them and **every other knotted partner**. These are **sibling threads**.

- **Visible** to the MC as thin red-gold threads linking the partners to each other.
- **Effects:**
  - Partners feel an **affinity** for each other: a predisposition to like, trust and understand each other. Rivals become friendly rivals. Strangers feel like old friends.
  - Partners can **sense each other** within about 100 meters.
  - Over time, many partners become friends, and some become lovers of each other (pink threads form alongside the sibling threads).
  - At 27 knots, sibling threads allow **Lending**: partners can borrow each other's Mitamashiro forms and powers (see 12).
- **Sibling threads do not remove jealousy.** They make it workable. Partners still compete for the MC's time and attention, still argue, and still have favorites. The household is a family, with a family's frictions.

## 6. Interfering With Threads

### Cutting (Enkiri)
- **Enkiri shears and blades,** consecrated to Tachikiri-no-Ōkami, can cut threads. The Hasami-gumi and the Kirigami-shū carry them.
- **Cutting an unlit thread:** impossible. Unlit ledger threads are inside the Shimenawa's protection.
- **Cutting a lit thread:** the clock jumps forward to **7 days remaining** (or stays where it is, if fewer than 7 remain). The ledger strand turns black-tipped.
- **Cutting a tied knot:** the knot is undone. The partner loses no blessings already received, but the bond breaks: sibling threads to that partner go dark, their Mitamashiro form is lost, and **a new 49-day clock starts**. The knot can be re-tied by a new sincere union. Being cut is painful for both: a sharp pain in the little finger and a sense of grief.
- **Mending:** at 54 knots, the MC can mend a cut thread within 49 days of the cut, restoring it without a new union.
- **Protection:** red silk cords spun by Tsumugi, worn on the little finger, make a thread harder to cut: the blade must be wielded by a Kishin-level cutter or by Tachikiri herself.

### Forging
- **Jorōgumo thread-forgers** can spin **counterfeit red threads**. A forged thread looks red to thread-sight and can be attached to a person to make them appear to be a ledger partner.
- **Purpose:** to get a faction's agent into the MC's household and bed. A forged partner would receive Grace, not a Knot, but could spy, influence or harm.
- **Detecting forgeries:** a forged thread has **no ember** when "lit," and it is attached at the wrist rather than the little finger, hidden under a sleeve. Tsumugi and Inaba can spot one immediately. The MC learns to spot them at 9 knots (by touch, a forged thread feels like nylon instead of silk).
- **Effect on the forged person:** wearing a forged thread for more than a few weeks produces kegare (+1 per day) and dreams of being strangled.

### The Curse of Coercion
Anyone who coerces a ledger partner or the MC into a union receives the **Curse of Severance**: every one of their own bonds turns grey over 49 days. Their family forgets them, their friends drift away, and they become a target for Kodoku. If they reach 100 kegare in this state, they become a Kodoku themselves. Only a god of matchmaking can lift the curse.

## 7. The Seal and the Bridge

Each knot tied pulls one orphaned thread taut through the Shimenawa and tears it out of the weave. The Shimenawa weakens with every knot.

| Knots tied | Effect on the Seal and the worlds |
|---|---|
| 1–8 | No visible effect. The Jingikan's instruments in the Sōmusubi vault register small tears. |
| 9 | The Veil weakens in Hachiōji. More people there glimpse the Ura. |
| 27 | The Veil weakens across Tokyo. Ordinary people occasionally see Magatsu, ghosts and gods, and the Jingikan's cleanup crews are overwhelmed. Old crossings at strong shrines begin to flicker at twilight. |
| 54 | **Half the load-bearing strands are gone.** Crossings at strong shrines reopen at twilight for anyone with intent. Kakuriyo's reiatsu begins seeping into Utsushiyo's sacred places. Nokori in Utsushiyo regain strength. Some humans with hidden heritage start to awaken without a Knot. |
| 81 | The Shimenawa weakens to its Tanabata level every new moon. Kamikakushi increase tenfold. The Veil is failing nationally. |
| 108 | **The Bridge.** The Shimenawa unravels. |

### Lapses and the Bridge
- Each lapsed thread is a load-bearing strand that will never be pulled out. The Shimenawa becomes impossible to unravel completely by knots alone.
- If any ledger thread lapses, **the Bridge cannot be completed by knots alone.** It requires the **Itomaki** (Tsumugi's spindle) to unspin the remainder by hand. This makes recovering the spindle from the Sōmusubi vault necessary for the endgame.

### What the Bridge Is
When the Shimenawa unravels, it does not simply vanish. The 108 knots and the Itomaki at the center become a **Bridge**: a permanent woven connection between the realms, anchored at Itokake Shrine.

- **The realms stay separate.** There is no merger. They are reconnected the way they were before 1906: overlapping, with crossings at shrines and crossroads at twilight.
- **The 1.2 million severed threads are released.** Those whose partners are still alive find their threads reconnecting. Over the following years, millions of cross-realm bonds re-form.
- **Reiatsu begins to equalize.** Utsushiyo's spiritual atmosphere thickens. Human magic grows stronger. Yokai in Utsushiyo recover.
- **The Veil fails** over about three years. Humanity learns that yokai and gods exist.
- **The Magatsu infestation can finally be fought:** Kakuriyo's natural predators can cross, purification restarts, and births rise.
- **Risks:** a disorderly opening (if the Itomaki is broken, or the Seal is torn rather than unspun) would cause a **reiatsu flood**: Kakuriyo's atmosphere rushing into Utsushiyo at once, waking every dormant heritage, causing mass reiyoi, a stampede of Magatsu and spiritual chaos. This is the disaster the Jingikan and the Kirigami-shū fear. The MC's knot-by-knot unraveling is the controlled version.

The endgame options are discussed in chapter 15.

## 8. The MC's Own Thread

The MC was born with an orphaned thread. Its far end leads into the Shimenawa. The Futa-musubi rite built the 108 hooks around it, but did not untie or re-tie it.

**Where it leads:** Tsumugi can trace it as far as the Itomaki in the Sōmusubi vault. The MC's own destined partner's end is **wound on the spindle itself**. Who it belongs to, and whether that person is alive, is unknown until the Itomaki is recovered. *(See chapter 17.)*
